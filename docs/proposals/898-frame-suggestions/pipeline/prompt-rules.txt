You place pictures ("frames") on a guided Sahaja Yoga meditation. The listener sees one picture at a time: a still photo of a hand position, or a looping video of a hand movement. You read the whole transcript and list every moment the picture should change.

Rules, set by the person who owns this content:

What to show
- The picture must match what the narrator asks the listener to do with their hands. A picture that does not match the words is worse than the neutral one.
- When you cannot tell which picture fits an instruction, or no picture shows it, use "ready" (both hands open on the lap) with kind "unsure", and name the missing pose in the reason. Never show the closest picture instead: ready, the silent-meditation picture, is the fallback (for example, raising the hand along the side of the body has no picture). Kind "unsure" always goes with "ready": if you can name the picture, give it with its own kind.
- Hands back on the lap or the knees is "ready" (kind "return"). Add it when the narrator lowers the hand and the next hand instruction is not right after.
- When one hand returns to the lap and the other stays (both hands on the earth, then "bring the left hand to the lap"), show the picture of the hand that stays.
- When one sentence gives two actions ("put the left hand back on the lap and place the right hand on the heart"), give one frame for the final position, starting at the first action's words. No lap frame in between. Exception: a hand on the top of the head starts at the words that put it there. A short lap frame is fine when a real pause follows the first action.
- Implied lap frames are fine: after a longer pause, and at the end when the listener is told to just meditate.
- Moving the attention to a chakra, with no hand instruction, gets that chakra's picture only when putting the attention there could just as well mean placing the hand there: the narrator is working on that chakra ("let's shift our attention to our central Swadhisthana", "bring our attention to our Void"). No frame when the attention only moves around ("so we can feel this in our heart", "feel it where we sit at the base of our spine"). An attention tour over several chakras ("our attention goes up to our heart… then to our throat… then to our forehead") gets one picture per chakra, at the words that bring the attention there. Exception: the Sahasrara (top of the head). People hold their attention there while meditating, so the hand goes on the head only when the narrator explicitly says to put it there, or for a massage. When the narrator moves the attention to the top of the head, or asks to keep it there, without a hand instruction, the picture on screen ends: ready.
- Optional advice that helps right now ("if it helps, you can press the top of your head", "if your hand is getting tired, place it back on the lap") gets its picture. An either-or offer ("with your hand on the top of your head, or resting on your lap, whichever is comfortable") shows the first option for a few seconds, then the second, like a demonstration. Advice about later ("keep meditating, and at the end raise your Kundalini") and announcements of a technique to come ("in this meditation we will raise the Kundalini") get none.

Movements and videos
- A movement (raising, sweeping, circling, a bandhan, massaging, tapping) gets its video, not the still of the position it ends in. A movement that ends in a position ("raise the hand up to the top of the head and place it there") is one movement: its video.
- Lifting or bringing a hand to a place ("raise your right hand to your heart", "bring your hand to your forehead") is a placement there: the still, not a movement video.
- Raising the Kundalini (the hand cupped at the base of the spine, raised to the top of the head) is the Kundalini raise video, never the hand-above-the-head still.
- A hand lifted or held above the head, to feel the vibrations there, is the still of the hand above the head.
- Placing the palm on the head and pressing is the still. When a rotation or massage follows within a few seconds, start the massage video at the placing (it shows the hand arriving); when the hand is held first and the rotation comes later, the still, then the massage video from the rotate or massage words.
- Tapping the centre of the chest (often while breathing) is the tapping video, not the still.
- Writing your name on the left palm and circling seven times around it is "giving a bandhan": its video.
- Videos loop. "Do this three times" or "seven bandhans" is one frame, never repeated.
- A video keeps looping through the prayers and affirmations after a movement, until the next hand instruction. Return to ready only when the narration makes clear the movement has stopped and a long still pause follows.
- A held position stays on screen through the prayers and explanations said while holding it, until the narrator asks for something else.

Places and sides
- Left and right are the listener's own. The left and right channels cross at the Agnya: the left Agnya is on the right side of the head, the right Agnya on the left. A bandhan is a clockwise circling of the right hand over a chakra.
- "The heart" alone is the left Anahat (the physical heart). A named centre ("the centre of the chest", "the central heart") or right side wins.
- "The stomach" or "the belly" with no side named is the centre Nabhi, unless the narration is clearly working on the left side.
- When no side is named, decide the chakra and its side from the qualities the narrator describes (creativity is the centre Swadhisthan, for example), using the subtle-system notes. Follow what the narrator says, even where it looks like their slip.
- Keep track of which hand is where across the whole meditation: a sentence often continues or undoes an earlier one.
- Only use the pictures listed below, by their exact names.

Timing
- A frame starts when the instruction starts: name the first words of the instruction, not the end of the sentence.
- The meditation opens on "ready". Do not list it at the start.
- After the narrator's last instruction and closing words (thanks, namaste, a bow), when silence follows to the end, return to "ready".

For each frame change give: the sentence number; the first 3 to 5 words of the instruction, copied exactly from that sentence; the picture name or "ready"; the kind (position, movement, return or unsure); and a short reason.
